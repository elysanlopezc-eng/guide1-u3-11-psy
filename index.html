What Makes People Different?

Human psychology seeks to explain why individuals think, feel, and behave in unique ways. While everyone shares fundamental biological structures, individual differences arise from a complex interplay between biological traits, moral choices, and environmental exposures.

Overall Blend

Personality

The dynamic sum of psychological traits

The unique and relatively enduring pattern of thinking, feeling, and acting that characterizes an individual across time and context.

Stability over time High (78%)
Biological Hardwiring

Temperament

Inborn emotional reactivity

The neurobiologically based disposition observable from infancy, governing emotional reactivity, arousal levels, and self-regulation.

Genetic Influence Very High (90%)
Moral & Ethical

Character

Acquired moral standards

The mental and moral qualities distinctive to an individual, shaped by personal choices, ethical values, self-discipline, and social norms.

Environmental Shaping High (85%)

Conceptual Distinction: Examples vs. Non-Examples

Concept Correct Example (Valid Instance) Non-Example (Common Misconception) Why it is a Non-Example
Temperament An infant crying intensely when exposed to sudden loud noises or bright lights. Choosing to donate money to a charitable foundation every month. Donating is a conscious moral choice (Character), not an innate physiological reaction.
Character Refusing to cheat on a exam even when nobody is watching. Having a naturally fast resting heart rate under mild stress. Heart rate reactivity is a biological response (Temperament), not an ethical virtue.
Personality Consistently being outgoing, talkative, and seeking social gatherings across high school. Feeling irritable for 20 minutes because you skipped breakfast. Temporary physical states (Mood/Hunger) are transient, whereas personality requires stability across time.
